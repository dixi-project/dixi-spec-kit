# Product roadmap

## Purpose

Show where the product is heading and why, in terms of outcomes rather than dates promised to the last day. The roadmap connects the business goal to the features that will be specified, so every item can be traced and questioned.

## Horizons

Use time horizons instead of fixed release dates. The further out an item is, the less detail and certainty it carries.

| Horizon | Meaning | Detail expected |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Now | Being specified or built | Linked feature spec with acceptance criteria |
| Next | Committed direction, not yet started | Problem statement, target outcome, rough size |
| Later | Ideas worth exploring | One line and the evidence that prompted it |

Rules:

- Every item states an outcome a user or the business would notice, not a task.
- Items in "Now" must point to a feature spec; items without one stay in "Next".
- Items enter and move between horizons through the [prioritization framework](prioritization-framework.md).
- Each item states which languages and markets it targets; an item that ships in only one language says so explicitly.

## Review cadence

- Review the roadmap at a fixed interval and after any significant research finding.
- Record each review: what moved, what was dropped and why.
- Communicate changes to the people who depend on the roadmap before publishing them.

## What the roadmap is not

- Not a delivery commitment: dates belong to release plans, not to the roadmap.
- Not a backlog: detailed tasks live in feature specs and the work tracker.
- Not a list of requests: every item needs evidence, not just a requester.
