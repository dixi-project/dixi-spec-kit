# Discovery interview guide

## Purpose

Give interviewers a reusable script for open conversations that uncover how people deal with a problem today, what triggers it and what matters to them, without leading them towards a solution. Adapt the questions per study.

## Before the session

- Confirm consent, recording permission and the participant's preferred language.
- Prepare the research questions this interview must inform.
- Assign roles: one interviewer, one note-taker.
- Test the call or room setup, including captions or interpreters if needed.

## Script (about 45 minutes)

### 1. Introduction (5 min)

> Thank you for your time. We are learning how people handle {{pending: topic}}. There are no right or wrong answers, and we are not testing you. You can skip any question or stop at any time. May we record this conversation for our notes only?

### 2. Context (5 min)

- Tell me a little about your role and a typical week.
- Where does {{pending: topic}} fit into that?

### 3. Last time it happened (20 min)

Ask about a specific, recent event rather than general habits.

- Tell me about the last time you had to {{pending: job}}. What was happening?
- What did you do first? And then?
- What tools, people or information did you use?
- What was hardest? Why?
- How did you know it was done well?
- What happened if it went wrong?

### 4. Alternatives and workarounds (10 min)

- Have you tried other ways to do this? What happened?
- What would make you change the way you do it today?
- What would stop you from changing?

### 5. Closing (5 min)

- Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
- Can we contact you again for follow-up?

## Interviewing rules

- Ask open questions; avoid "would you use…" and "do you like…".
- Ask for stories and examples, then dig with "why" and "tell me more".
- Stay silent after a question; let the participant fill the pause.
- Do not pitch, explain or defend the product.
- Write down exact words for important quotes.

## After the session

- Write a summary within 24 hours: key observations, quotes, surprises.
- File notes in the research log with the participant anonymized.
